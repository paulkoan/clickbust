The AI infrastructure spree is slowing

02 November 2026

The numbers coming in from the big cloud providers show a clear pattern. Capex has been ramping for two years straight. But the last round of earnings calls had a different tone. Executives started talking about efficiency. They mentioned optimising spend. That is code for the party ending. Power constraints are real. Data centre build times are stretching. And the returns on that spending are not obvious yet. The revenue from AI services is growing, but not at the same rate as the investment. This gap is getting harder to ignore.

Some analysts are calling for a correction. They point to the astronomical valuations of GPU makers. They note that enterprise adoption has been slower than hype suggested. The real AI use cases are still narrow. Most companies are experimenting, not deploying at scale. The infrastructure build out assumed demand that has not materialised. That does not mean the technology is worthless. It means the capital allocation was ahead of reality.

The UK market is not immune. We have our own data centre boom. But the same logic applies. If the big US players pull back, the supply chain here feels it too. This is not a crash. It is a repricing. The bubble is not bursting, but it is deflating. The smart money is watching utilisation rates. When they plateau, the spending will follow.
