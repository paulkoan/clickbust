Local LLMs are for boring automation tasks

01 October 2026

The useful local LLM work is not chat. It is the dull, repetitive stuff where you already know the shape of the answer. Extracting fields from invoices, sorting support tickets into categories, rewriting error messages into plain English. That kind of thing.

The real advantages are cost and privacy. Once you are pushing a few thousand requests a day, the API bill stops being trivial. And some data should not leave the machine. Patient records, internal financials, unreleased product specs. A local model keeps those where they belong.

But the models are not magic. A 7B parameter model will not reason as well as a frontier API. So do not build your automation around what the model can do in a demo. Build it around what it can do reliably at 2am with no supervision. Structured output, JSON schemas, classification. If the task sits near the edge of what a regex can handle but needs a bit of flexibility, that is the sweet spot.

The trap is fine-tuning. Most people do not need it. Start with a good quantised model and a well-written prompt. If that fails, try a bigger model. If that fails, then think about fine-tuning. In practice, the bottleneck is almost never the model. It is your prompt and your data.

Run the model on a decent GPU. A Mac with enough unified memory works. A dedicated Nvidia card works. Anything else
