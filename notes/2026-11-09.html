The AI Infrastructure Bet Is Getting Harder to Justify

09 November 2026

The numbers are staggering. Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta are spending over a quarter of a trillion dollars a year on AI infrastructure combined. This is the biggest capital buildout in tech history. The justification rests on a single bet: that demand for AI compute will keep outpacing supply.

The cracks are showing. Enterprise revenue from AI services is growing, but it is not growing fast enough to cover the hardware and energy bills. The hyperscalers are reporting rising capex and cautious guidance on the revenue side. Shareholders are starting to ask when the spending translates into profit, not just technical capability.

The biggest variable is efficiency. The scaling laws that drove the first wave of spending are under pressure. New architectures, better training techniques, and cheaper inference mean you can do more with less. This is good for adoption, but it undermines the case for buying millions of GPUs on the assumption that scarcity persists. The Jevons paradox argument says cheaper compute creates more demand. That might hold, but it is a theory, not a guarantee.

The bubble is not bursting today. No one is pulling the plug. But the mood has shifted. The blank cheque era is closing. Every new data centre announcement now comes with a tougher question about payback. That is a healthy sign, but it is a real constraint. The buildout continues, but the margin for error is shrinking.
