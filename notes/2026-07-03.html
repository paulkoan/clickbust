The Deal Hunting Trap

03 July 2026

Spent half an hour this morning watching a bloke on YouTube explain how to save 12 quid on a train ticket by booking six weeks in advance and splitting the journey at a station you don't even get off at. He was dead serious. And I thought, that's 12 quid for half an hour of your life plus the mental overhead of remembering to do it. That's not a saving. That's a part-time job with a terrible hourly rate.

Value shopping is the thing everyone thinks they should do. But most people confuse it with cheapness. Cheap is the lowest price. Value is the best ratio of quality to cost. And the hunt for cheap often destroys value in ways you don't see. The time you spend comparing two kettles that are 4 quid apart could be spent earning the difference. Or just resting. Or reading something worthwhile.

The real trick is to stop treating shopping as a hobby. If you need a kettle, buy a decent one once. Don't spend three evenings reading reviews of 20 quid kettles that'll break in a year. That's not thrift. That's exhaustion.

I saw a statistic once that said the average person spends 30 minutes a day looking for deals. That's 182 hours a year. You could learn a language in that time. Or fix your own bike. Or just sit in the garden and stare at the sky. But instead we're all in a spreadsheet comparing unit prices on washing-up liquid.

The best value move I've seen is simple. Buy good stuff. Use it until it falls apart. Replace it with the same thing. That's it. No hunting. No apps. No alerts. Just a quiet life with a decent kettle.
