The Same Stories Keep Selling

26 August 2026

Every new show or movie gets the same treatment. The marketing points to a scandal, a feud, a shocking behind the scenes story. The headlines promise something explosive. But when you watch the thing, it's never that simple. The clickbait version is always better than the real story.

These patterns work because they offer a shortcut to drama. You don't need to sit through a film to feel like you know what really happened. The clip, the quote, the unnamed source gives you a version you can form an opinion about in ten seconds. That is the product. Not the entertainment itself but the feeling of being in the know.

The industry keeps producing this because we keep sharing it. A headline about a star storming off set gets more engagement than a thoughtful review of their performance. That is a fact of the system. The platforms reward the reactive over the reflective.

None of this is new. But the speed is. A film can be reduced to a single gossip piece before it even opens. The actual work becomes secondary to the manufactured drama around it. If you find yourself reading another expose about a tantrum or a rift, ask what is being sold. It is rarely the story itself. It is your attention.
